Design Computation (DC) is an approach in architecture, engineering,
and construction that leverages computation to generate building
designs, manage the associated data, and evaluate design decisions.
DC differs from CAD-based design modelling, where the designer uses
interactive geometry applications to manually create the finished
building design. In Design Computation, however, programs are written
to generate the designs themselves as their output, explicitly
programming the logic and method of their realisation, and enabling
design intent to be more easily captured.

At the heart of DC is programming, which must then be expressed in
some programming language or languages. Both textual and visual
programming languages are widely deployed in this space; some of these
are general purpose and others are specialised, used only in these
domains. This PhD project responds to the challenge: how can the unique
ways of collaborative thinking and reasoning, which characterise the
multi-disciplinary design of physical architecture and building
engineering, be supported by new forms of programming languages,
programming systems, and programming tools?

I published a position paper at Onward! 2024 about these ideas with the
external collaborators on this project, who are experts in the design
computation side (in industry and academia):

> [Programming languages for the future of design computation](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3689492.3689812). Onward! 2024.
